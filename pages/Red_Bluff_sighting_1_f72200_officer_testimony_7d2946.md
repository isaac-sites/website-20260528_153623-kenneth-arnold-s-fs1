## Introduction

In the **[Red Bluff sighting]({{ 'red-bluff-sighting-1960/' | relative_url }}) (1960)**, the testimony of the two California Highway Patrol officers at the centre of the case — **Charles A. Carson** and **Stanley E. Scott** — has been the focal point of both serious interest and sustained debate. Their reported encounter on the night of **13 August 1960** is widely cited in UFO literature not simply because something unusual was observed, but because the observers were trained, credible law enforcement professionals whose detailed narrative and documentation distinguished this case from many civilian reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[nicap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO</span></span></span>

## Who Carson and Scott Were

Charles A. Carson and Stanley E. Scott were experienced officers with the California Highway Patrol (CHP) when they came across an extraordinary aerial phenomenon while on routine patrol on Hoag Road, east of Corning, California. Their professional background as patrolmen meant they were accustomed to observing vehicles and aircraft and making rapid, precise assessments in the field. This professional grounding — coupled with documented service records affirming their reliability within the CHP hierarchy — underpins why many researchers have treated their accounts with particular seriousness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/ufosymposium/harder.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[files.ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Symposium 1968: Harder Statement...</span></span></span>

Their report was formalised in a **written statement to the CHP Area Commander**, included intact in subsequent investigations, and was supplemented by interviews conducted by independent researchers such as Dr James E. McDonald and NICAP representatives. This preserved primary account — rather than second‑hand recollection — is one reason the incident is classed among higher‑credibility UFO reports.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.saturdaynightuforia.com/html/articles/articlehtml/redbluff-1960.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saturdaynightuforia.com">[saturdaynightuforia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saturdaynightuforia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1960The Radar Base confirmed the U.F.O. - completely unidentified. ## What They Reported Seeing

According to Carson and Scott’s teletype report, which was later incorporated into civilian investigations, the encounter began shortly before midnight on 13 August 1960. They initially perceived what appeared to be a descending aircraft about to crash in front of them. Exiting their patrol vehicle, they observed **a silent, glowing object descend to approximately 100–200 feet above the ground before abruptly reversing and climbing to about 500 feet**, where it hovered in place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/ufoe/section_7.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[nicap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Section 7Examples abound of cases in which police responded to citizens&#x27; reports of UFOs, and saw the objects for themselves...</span></span></span>

Their description — consistent across their own written report and later interviews — notes that the object was **surrounded by a glow**, had **distinct red lights at each end**, and intermittently displayed **white lights between the reds**. They reported that it performed **rapid and unexpected manoeuvres** that did not align with known aircraft behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[nicap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO Case DirectoryImage Category 3 Electromagnetic Effects Red Bluff Incident Red Bluff, California August 13, 1960 Richard Hal...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1960</span></span></span>

Importantly, they described **radio interference** coinciding with the object’s proximity and an unusual **red beam** that appeared to sweep over the ground and their patrol car at intervals. These elements — electromagnetic effects and dynamic movement — added layers to the narrative that went beyond mere visual observation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://xufos.com/red-bluff-ufo-1960.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xufos.com">[xufos.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xufos.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

The officers also emphasised their attempts to rationalise what they saw. Both had some prior exposure to aerial phenomena: Carson had military service and Scott served as a paratrooper during the Korean War. In later statements to researchers, they rejected common atmospheric [explanations]({{ 'explanations-3a7447/' | relative_url }}) put forward by detractors, noting familiarity with effects like the northern lights and weather balloons and asserting that their observations could not be satisfactorily explained by such phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/clarb60.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Red Bluff case, USA, August 196018 Oct 2003 — Carson and Scott talked with several deputies at the Tehama County She...</span></span></span>

## Why Trained Witnesses Changed the Case

### Credibility Anchored in Law Enforcement Professionalism

In UFO case files, **police testimony is often weighted more heavily than casual civilian reports** because officers are presumed to have observational experience, awareness of conventional aircraft, and a discipline in documentation. In this context, Carson and Scott’s **official teletype report to the CHP chain of command** — rather than a retrospective anecdote — positioned the Red Bluff incident as more than passing eyewitness testimony.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.saturdaynightuforia.com/html/articles/articlehtml/redbluff-1960.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saturdaynightuforia.com">[saturdaynightuforia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saturdaynightuforia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1960The Radar Base confirmed the U.F.O. - completely unidentified. Moreover, external investigators who examined the case, including those associated with civilian research organisations like NICAP and UFO researchers such as James E. McDonald, explicitly regarded the officers as **credible and composed** observers whose detailed sequence of events held evidentiary value. In interviews years later, supervisors noted the officers were not inclined toward publicity, further reinforcing assessments of their trustworthiness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/ufosymposium/hall.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[files.ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">In some of these cases, no familiar explan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Symposium 1968: Hall StatementIn my judgment there are many reports of UFOs that meet the above criteria quite well -- better, indeed, th...</span></span></span>

### Corroborating Observations

Carson and Scott’s narrative gains additional credibility from the presence of **multiple corroborating witnesses**. Tehama County Sheriff’s deputies who responded to the reported sighting described seeing the object(s) from a different vantage point with similar light patterns. A local night jailer was also documented as observing the object, and independent civilian sightings of unusual lights that night were reported in local press accounts.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.saturdaynightuforia.com/html/articles/articlehtml/redbluff-1960.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saturdaynightuforia.com">[saturdaynightuforia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saturdaynightuforia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1960The Radar Base confirmed the U.F.O. - completely unidentified. This network of independent observers with consistent impressions strengthens the impression that the officers were not isolated misperceivers but part of a broader pattern of observation that night.

### Scientific and Official Response

When the incident was taken under review by the United States Air Force’s official UFO investigation programme, **Project Blue Book**, the officers’ testimony was included in the case file. However, the Air Force ultimately offered a conventional explanation — largely attributing the sightings to astronomical refraction phenomena — that was contested by civilian researchers and some scientific commentators at the time for underestimating the detailed visual and behavioural elements described by the officers.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.saturdaynightuforia.com/html/articles/articlehtml/redbluff-1960.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saturdaynightuforia.com">[saturdaynightuforia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saturdaynightuforia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1960The Radar Base confirmed the U.F.O. - completely unidentified. McDonald — a respected atmospheric physicist engaged in evaluating UFO reports — considered cases with trained observers like Carson and Scott to be among the stronger “unknowns” because misidentification by professionals was deemed less likely.
